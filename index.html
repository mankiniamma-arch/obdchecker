Quick US Driver FAQs

Is it safe to drive with my Check Engine Light on?

If the Check Engine Light is steady (solid yellow/amber) and the car drives normally, you can usually drive carefully and schedule a check within a few days. If the Check Engine Light is flashing, pull over safely right away—a flashing light means active engine misfires that can quickly destroy your catalytic converter.

Where can I get my OBD-II trouble code scanned for free in the US?

Most major US auto parts stores—including AutoZone, O'Reilly Auto Parts, Advance Auto Parts, and NAPA—offer free OBD-II code scans in their parking lots. Enter the printed code (like P0420 or P0171) into this checker for an instant safety verdict and US repair cost range.

Why did my Check Engine Light turn on right after getting gas?

A loose, cracked, or unclicked gas cap is the #1 zero-dollar cause of EVAP codes like P0455, P0442, and P0456. Tighten the cap until it clicks 2 to 3 times; the light usually resets automatically after 2 to 5 normal drives.

Which dashboard warning lights mean I must stop driving immediately?

Red warning lights—including Low Oil Pressure (oil can), Engine Coolant Overheating (thermometer), Brake System Warning (when the parking brake is off), Battery/Charging failure, and a Flashing Check Engine Light—mean you should pull over safely and turn off the engine to avoid severe damage or brake loss.

How accurate are the US repair cost ranges shown here?

Repair ranges reflect typical US independent shop and dealership parts-plus-labor pricing nationwide. Actual quotes vary by vehicle make, model, local labor rates, and whether a $0 quick check solves the issue first.
